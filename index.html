    THE VILLAGE MODEL is a fictional strategy game about imitation, inspired by Rene Girard's mimetic theory. It is a model of a system, with all the limits that implies, not a guide to handling real people. Every intervention can fail.     THE VILLAGE
    MODEL

    Twelve weeks in an isolated village that runs on imitation. You cannot order people to be good. You can only choose what gets seen, praised, funded, and blamed, and then live with what spreads. Keep the services running and the trust alive until the winter count. Output bought with fear gets the ending it earned.

    Choose the posting charter

    Choose the season

    Every season shuffles the order of events, jitters the starting ledger, and lets crises write their own weeks. No two villages imitate alike.

    How the village works

    People here do not invent most of their desires. They borrow them. A resident watches someone they admire, sees what that person wants and how they act, and starts wanting it too. That is imitation, and it is neither good nor bad by itself. It is just how this village runs.

    Models are made, not born

    Anyone whose visibility reaches 60 becomes a model: their stance starts spreading to the people who admire them. Mara, Jonah, and Priya start near that line. Public recognition pushes visibility up. Blame, strain, and neglect push it down, and a model who drops under 30 stops being watched. Elevate the careful and you get a careful village. Elevate the cornered and you get corner-cutting at scale.

    Rivalry

    When many people copy the same desire and the prize is scarce (one banner, first place in a queue, the only oven slot), imitation turns into rivalry. The model stops being an example and becomes an obstacle.

    Shared projects and Custom

    A shared project points imitation at cooperation instead of a prize. Hold trust and cohesion steady week after week and the village builds Custom: a streak that absorbs the first points of trust and cohesion damage. Habits, once formed, protect themselves.

    Scapegoating

    Blaming one resident for a shared failure works, briefly. Tension drops because everyone else agrees on whose fault it is. Then the bill arrives: trust falls, the targeted person's wellbeing falls, they withdraw, and the village learns that blame is how problems get solved here. Use it three times and the ending writes itself.

    Crises write their own weeks

    Let scarcity or rivalry run past 65 and the season interrupts your plans with a crisis week. Let a resident's wellbeing collapse and the village notices the empty chair. The ledger is not decoration. It is the schedule.

    Uncertainty

    No intervention guarantees its result. Risky choices name the stat they ride on. High trust makes open books work. Low cohesion makes shared crews fragile. The dice are visible in the outcome text, never hidden.

    Winning

    There is no score to maximize. At the winter count the village is judged whole: services running, trust intact, people still choosing their own norms. Commendations record how you did it, not just that you did.

    Paused

    The village holds its breath. Nothing moves while you think.
